## Before the Cold Sets In: A Pre-Winter Ceiling Check for Bergen County Homeowners

If you've lived in Bergen County for more than a few winters, you already know what's coming. The temperatures will swing from mild to bitter, the heating system will kick on for the first time in months, and somewhere in your house — probably in a ceiling corner or along a wall seam — something is going to crack.

That's not pessimism. That's just New Jersey in November.

Doing a thorough pre-winter ceiling check in Bergen County isn't something most homeowners think about until there's already a problem. But a little time spent inspecting your walls and ceilings before the cold weather locks in can save you from expensive repairs down the road — and help you catch issues that, left alone, tend to get much worse once the heat comes on and the freeze-thaw cycle begins.

This checklist is designed to help you know what to look for, what's worth worrying about, and when it's time to call in a professional.

---

## How This Season Affects Your Walls and Ceilings

Bergen County sits in a part of New Jersey that experiences some of the most punishing seasonal transitions on the East Coast. Autumn here isn't just a few cool weeks — it's a rapid shift from humid summer air to dry winter cold, often within just a few weeks.

Here's why that matters for your drywall and plaster:

**Humidity swings.** In summer, indoor humidity levels in Bergen County homes can hover between 60–70%. Come winter, with the heat running full blast, that number can drop below 30%. Drywall and plaster absorb and release moisture as conditions change, and that constant movement causes expansion and contraction. Over time, this leads to cracking — especially along joints and seams.

**The freeze-thaw cycle.** This is a big one for older homes, particularly in towns like Ridgewood, Teaneck, and Hackensack where you'll find houses built in the 1920s through 1950s. Any moisture that has worked its way into exterior walls — from a small roof leak, a failing window seal, or poor flashing — will freeze, expand, and then thaw repeatedly throughout winter. The result is damage that compounds quickly and often shows up as bubbling paint, soft spots in drywall, or cracked plaster.

**Heating system activation.** When forced-air heating kicks on for the first time each fall, it circulates dry air rapidly through your home. This sudden change accelerates the drying and shrinking of building materials. Don't be surprised if hairline cracks appear in the weeks after you first fire up the furnace.

## Things to Check This Season

Walk through your home with fresh eyes. Here's a practical checklist to guide your inspection:

### 1. Look for Cracks Along Ceiling Seams and Corners
Run your eyes along the edges where walls meet ceilings. Hairline cracks here are often cosmetic and result from seasonal movement. However, cracks that are wider than 1/8 inch, jagged, or that appear suddenly can indicate something more serious, like settling or structural movement.

### 2. Check Around Windows and Exterior Doors
Water infiltration almost always shows up near windows first. Look for discoloration, bubbling paint, or soft spots in the drywall surrounding window frames. In Fort Lee and areas close to the Hudson, homes are particularly exposed to wind-driven rain that can push water past imperfect seals.

### 3. Inspect Ceilings Below Bathrooms and Kitchens
Upstairs bathrooms are a common source of moisture that works its way into ceilings. Check the ceiling directly below any bathroom or kitchen for staining, sagging, or soft areas. Even a small slow leak from a toilet wax ring or supply line can cause significant [ceiling damage](/) over a season.

### 4. Tap Along Plaster Walls in Older Homes
If you live in a home with original plaster walls — common throughout older Bergen County neighborhoods — do a simple tap test. A hollow sound in an area that used to sound solid means the plaster is separating from the lath beneath it. This is a fall repair, not a spring repair.

### 5. Look at Your Attic Hatch and Any Drop Ceiling Panels
Attic hatches are notorious for showing early signs of moisture problems. Discolored panels or warped tiles are telling you that condensation or a roof leak is present. Don't ignore it.

### 6. Note Any Paint That's Peeling or Bubbling Indoors
Interior paint that's peeling — especially on ceilings — almost always means moisture is involved. This isn't just a cosmetic issue; it's a symptom.

## Common Seasonal Drywall Problems

Even well-maintained homes develop issues heading into winter. Here are the most common ones we see across Bergen County:

### Hairline Cracks from Thermal Movement
These are the most frequent calls we get in October and November. As the heat turns on and humidity drops, drywall contracts slightly, opening up cracks along taped joints. Most of these are superficial, but they should be properly repaired rather than just painted over. A correct [drywall repair](/services/drywall-repair/) involves re-taping, feathering compound, and texture matching — not just a coat of paint.

### Water Stains from Summer Roof Leaks
Summer storms reveal roof vulnerabilities that often don't get repaired before fall. By the time you notice a brown ring on your ceiling in November, the damage beneath the surface may already be significant. That stained area may have compromised drywall paper, mold beginning to form, or insulation that's been saturated.

### Plaster Delamination
In Bergen County's older housing stock, plaster delamination is a genuine concern every winter. Once the plaster loses its bond with the lath, it becomes a safety issue — not just a cosmetic one. If you're hearing hollow sounds or seeing long diagonal cracks in plaster walls, it's worth having a professional take a look at [plaster repair](/services/plaster-repair/) options before winter sets in.

### Popcorn Ceiling Cracking and Separation
Old popcorn texture is especially vulnerable to humidity changes. As it dries out in winter, the texture can crack and even fall in sections. If your home still has popcorn ceilings, fall is a good time to consider whether [popcorn ceiling removal](/services/popcorn-ceiling-removal/) makes more sense than ongoing patching.

## How to Protect Your Home This Winter

Prevention isn't complicated, but it does require being proactive:

- **Control indoor humidity.** Use a whole-home humidifier or room humidifiers to keep indoor humidity between 35–45% during winter. This reduces the expansion/contraction cycle that causes cracking.
- **Address roof and flashing issues before December.** Any known roof vulnerability should be fixed before freeze-thaw cycles begin. Water that gets in during a November rain can freeze in December and cause significantly more damage.
- **Seal window and door frames.** Check caulking around all exterior-facing windows and doors. Deteriorated caulk is an easy fix that prevents major moisture intrusion.
- **Don't paint over cracks without repairing them properly.** Paint is not a patch. Cracks that are painted over without proper compound and tape will reopen, often larger, within a season.
- **Schedule repairs before the holidays.** Professional drywall repairs require time to cure properly, and scheduling in October or November means the work is done before family gatherings fill your calendar in December.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Are small ceiling cracks something I should worry about before winter?**

A: It depends on the crack. Hairline cracks along painted seams are usually cosmetic and result from normal seasonal movement. However, cracks that are wider than 1/8 inch, that run diagonally, or that appear suddenly alongside other symptoms (staining, soft spots, sagging) warrant a professional evaluation. When in doubt, a free estimate costs you nothing.

**Q: How do I know if a ceiling stain is from an old leak or an active one?**

A: Feel the drywall around and behind the stain. If it feels soft, spongy, or cool to the touch, the moisture may still be present. A dry, hard surface with a brown ring is more likely an old stain. That said, even "old" stains should be properly repaired — the paper facing of drywall that's been wet is permanently weakened and can harbor mold.

**Q: Is it too cold to do drywall repairs in winter?**

A: Inside your home, temperature isn't generally the issue — joint compound needs to be applied and cured in spaces above 55°F, which any heated home will maintain. Winter is actually a great time to schedule interior drywall work since contractors' schedules are often more flexible than in spring and summer.

**Q: My plaster walls make a hollow sound in one spot. Is that dangerous?**

A: It can be. Delaminated plaster — particularly on ceilings — can fall without much warning. If you're hearing hollow sounds overhead, don't wait. This is one situation where prompt professional assessment is genuinely important, especially in homes with heavy original plaster construction.
